## Section 4 — Per-Program Deep Dives

### 4.1 McMaster BHSc

**Where Justin sits:** 27th percentile of the 2025-26 accepted pool (95.5% vs median 96.8%). On GPA alone this is a reach. With very high EC weight applied, the model upgrades to Target — but that upgrade is contingent on a strong supplementary application.

**The rejection median is a wake-up call:** With the full May rejection wave in, McMaster's 2025-26 rejection median is **95.5%** — identical to Justin's projected average. He is sitting exactly at the line between who got in and who didn't. This is not a reason for panic; supp app quality is a real differentiator here (students with 82.0%–95% were accepted, students with 97%+ were rejected). But it makes the ELA 30-1 target non-negotiable.

**OOP reality:** This is the one program where being from Alberta genuinely matters. All 4 OOP accepted applicants in 2025-26 had averages of 97.0%, 97.5%, 97.8%, and 99.83%. Both OOP rejections were at 93.8% and **98.0%** — the 98.0% rejection is the most important data point in the entire dataset for Justin: a student with nearly a perfect average was rejected. This confirms the OOP bar at McMaster is not purely about hitting a GPA floor — supp app quality is decisive even at high averages. At Justin's current 95.5% midpoint, he is below every observed OOP acceptance. **This gap disappears if ELA 30-1 reaches 92-93%, pushing his top-6 to 97.0–97.3%.**

**What the supp app rewards:** McMaster's format is 2 written essays (1,500 characters each, untimed), scored 0–7 by 6 independent reviewers who spend 45–60 seconds per response. The questions are deliberately provocative analytical prompts — not personal statements. The data is clear: applicants with 98-99% GPAs get rejected when their responses are generic. Applicants with 90-95% GPAs get accepted when their reasoning is specific, unusual, and clearly their own. The 2025-26 questions were:
- **Q1A:** Design new criteria to replace GPA — justify your proposal
- **Q1B:** If university research faced cuts, which area first? Defend your choice
- **Q2A:** Evaluate a biotech NeuroPatch device for high school testing — benefits, risks, safeguards
- **Q2B:** Analyze government data on school performance — critique validity, identify gaps

**Justin's ideal questions (if 2026-27 follows the same format):**
- **Q1A** maps perfectly to his IB CS IA: he literally built an alternative student assessment system (ML survey-based risk detection). His response doesn't hypothesize — it describes a real system, motivated by a real case (his classmate), validated through real ML competition experience.
- **Q2A** maps to Harvard MEDScience: he will have spent a week learning the biodesign process and evaluating AI surgical devices. No other applicant will have this exact credential for this exact type of question.

**Specific gaps for McMaster:**
1. GPA must reach 97.0%+ for OOP viability (ELA 30-1 is the lever)
2. Supp app drafts not started — Q1A and Q2A responses need October drafts and multiple iterations
3. Harvard MEDScience story needs to be captured in writing immediately after June 26

**Top 3 actions:**
1. Push ELA 30-1 to 92-93% — this is the OOP gate
2. Journal the Harvard MEDScience biodesign experience in detail on June 27-28
3. Draft Q1A response by October (with IB CS IA as the anchor); draft Q2A after Harvard MEDScience

### 4.2 Queen's BHSc

**Where Justin sits:** 38th percentile of the 2025-26 accepted pool (95.5% vs acceptance median 95.9%). This is the most accessible Tier 1 program for Justin — the GPA gap is smaller, and critically, there is **no observed OOP penalty**. Four OOP students were accepted in 2025-26 with averages of 93.5%, 95.0%, 96.2%, and 97.55% — Justin's 95.5% midpoint sits within this range.

**The waitlist finding changes the framing:** With the full May cycle data in, Queen's BHSc issued 13 waitlists in May with a median GPA of **96.5%** — meaningfully higher than the acceptance median of 95.9%. This is the most counterintuitive finding from the 2025-26 data: Queen's is waitlisting stronger-GPA students and admitting weaker-GPA students. The only explanation is PSE quality. Students who performed well on KIRA went straight to acceptance; students with higher GPAs but weaker PSEs landed on the waitlist. Justin at 95.5% is below the waitlist median — which means his GPA is not what will put him on the waitlist. **His PSE performance will determine whether he's in the accept pool or the waitlist pool.**

**Supp app format — KIRA Talent:** Queen's BHSc PSE is conducted through KIRA Talent and has two components:
- **Written:** 335 words in 10 minutes (timed). This rewards authentic, fast thinking — not polished rhetoric.
- **Video:** 2 minutes of preparation + 2 minutes of delivery. One prompt, one shot.

All 44 accepted applicants in 2025-26 completed KIRA. The data shows a wide acceptance range (82.5%–99.7%) and relatively few outright rejections (n=9, all at ≤94.8%), suggesting Queen's BHSc weighs the PSE heavily enough to admit students whose GPAs are below the norm if the PSE is strong. A BJJ black belt instructor with a predicted IB of 34/45 was accepted at 82.5%.

**What the PSE rewards:** Personal narrative, authenticity, and the "why medicine / why health sciences" story. This is the inverse of McMaster — Queen's wants to hear about Justin's journey, not just his analytical arguments. His classmate → HelpingMinds → Chatter → clinical shadowing arc is directly what the PSE asks for. The video component rewards natural delivery and genuine emotion, not performance.

**Key risk for Justin:** The 10-minute timed written component catches students who have not practiced. At 335 words in 10 minutes (33 words/minute), it is faster than most people's comfortable drafting pace under pressure. Practice sessions with a timer are non-negotiable.

**Specific gaps for Queen's BHSc:**
1. No KIRA video practice — this is a learnable skill that requires repetition, not talent
2. Written PSE not timed — Justin needs to practice the 335-word / 10-minute constraint specifically
3. Classmate story needs to be crystallized so it flows naturally in a timed format

**Top 3 actions:**
1. Record 3-5 mock KIRA video responses (2 min prep / 2 min delivery) — watch them back critically
2. Do 5+ timed written practice runs (335 words, 10 min) on health science prompts
3. Crystallize the classmate story to a natural 90-second spoken version by October

## Section 8 — Data Caveats

This report is grounded in real applicant data but draws from a self-selected population. Four specific caveats apply:

1. **Acceptance bias:** The Reddit dataset is approximately 95%+ accepted applicants. Rejected applicants systematically under-report. This means all GPA percentiles in this report are calculated relative to the admitted population, not the full applicant pool. The true admission rate at each school is much lower than the Reddit data implies.

2. **Small OOP samples:** McMaster BHSc OOP data (n=4 accepted, n=2 rejected) and Queen's BHSc OOP data (n=4 accepted) are small. The observed OOP floor at McMaster (~97.0%) could shift meaningfully with one more data point in either direction. Treat it as a directional signal, not a hard threshold.

3. **Year-to-year variation:** The 2026-27 supp app questions at McMaster and Queen's will be different from 2025-26. The format and scoring rubric are stable; the specific prompts change annually. Justin must prepare for the form of these questions, not memorize responses to 2025-26 prompts.

4. **Self-reported data quality:** GPA figures are self-reported by Reddit users, some of whom may be imprecise about which average (top-6, top-4, all subjects) they cited. The analysis normalizes for this where possible, but edge cases exist.

Despite these caveats, the dataset is large enough (10,303 rows, 4 cycles) that the directional signals are robust: the Tier 1 admission medians, the OOP patterns, and the supp app quality differentials are consistent across cycles and are reliable inputs for strategic planning.

**Small OOP samples (updated):** McMaster BHSc now has n=4 OOP accepted, n=2 OOP rejected. The 2 OOP rejections (93.8% and 98.0%) confirm the OOP bar is not purely GPA-driven; supp app quality matters. Queen's BHSc OOP (n=4 accepted, 1 waitlisted) shows no GPA-based penalty.
